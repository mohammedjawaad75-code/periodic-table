CHEMISTRY LEARNING CENTRE

Understand the fundamental concepts behind the periodic table.

⚛️

ATOMIC STRUCTURE

Every atom consists of a nucleus containing positively charged protons and neutral neutrons. Negatively charged electrons occupy regions around the nucleus called electron shells and orbitals. The number of protons determines the identity of an element.

🔢

ATOMIC NUMBER

Atomic number is the number of protons present in the nucleus of an atom. Hydrogen has atomic number 1 because it contains one proton, while oxygen has atomic number 8 because it contains eight protons.

⚖️

ATOMIC MASS

Atomic mass is the weighted average mass of an element's naturally occurring isotopes. It is expressed in unified atomic mass units (u). Protons and neutrons account for nearly all the mass of an atom.

📊

GROUPS AND PERIODS

The periodic table contains 18 vertical columns called groups and 7 horizontal rows called periods. Elements in the same group often share similar chemical properties because they have related outer electron configurations.

🔬

ISOTOPES

Isotopes are atoms of the same element with equal numbers of protons but different numbers of neutrons. For example, carbon-12 and carbon-14 are isotopes of carbon.

⚡

ELECTRON CONFIGURATION

Electron configuration describes how electrons are arranged in atomic orbitals. This arrangement helps explain an element's chemical behaviour, bonding and position in the periodic table.

🧲

PERIODIC TRENDS

Atomic radius, electronegativity and ionization energy show patterns across periods and down groups. These trends help scientists predict chemical behaviour and compare elements.

🧪

CHEMICAL BONDING

Atoms combine to form compounds through chemical bonds. Ionic bonding involves electron transfer, covalent bonding involves electron sharing, and metallic bonding involves delocalized electrons.

🌌

ORIGIN OF ELEMENTS

Many elements were created through stellar nucleosynthesis. Hydrogen and helium formed in the early universe, while stars and stellar explosions helped create many heavier elements. Scientists also synthesize elements in laboratories.
